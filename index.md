## ✨ Key Features in Plain Language

**Versioned research plans**  
Your experiment plan is saved like a document with a history. If you change your mind, you can look back at earlier versions. Nothing is lost, and you always know which plan was used for a run.

**Dependency maps**  
Some steps must happen before others. laborix draws a map so you can see the order at a glance. It prevents you from starting a step too early and helps you understand what is waiting on what.

**Pause and resume**  
Long experiments do not need to run in one sitting. You can pause laborix, turn off your computer, and continue later. The application picks up exactly where it left off, so you do not repeat work.

**Tamper-evident record book**  
Every action is written into a record that shows what happened and when. If something changes unexpectedly, the record makes it easy to notice. This is helpful when you need to trust your results.

**Smart experiment chooser**  
When you have several experiments to try, laborix can suggest which one to run next. It balances trying new things with sticking with what has worked. The application also measures how well these suggestions perform against known examples, so you can see the benefit.

**Ablation bookkeeping**  
If you remove or change one part of an experiment to see its effect, laborix keeps track of those variations. You do not need to manage a separate spreadsheet.

**Offline reports**  
laborix creates summary reports on your own computer. You do not need an internet connection to generate or read them.

**Runs on a normal computer**  
The core of laborix uses NumPy, a common number-crunching tool. An optional extra uses a CPU-only version of torch. No special graphics card is required.

## 🧪 Your First Experiment in Five Steps

1. **Create a plan.** Click “New Plan” and give it a name. Describe what you want to test in everyday words. laborix saves this as your versioned plan.
2. **Add steps.** List the steps of your experiment. For each step, note whether it must wait for another step. laborix builds the dependency map for you.
3. **Choose a chooser.** If you have multiple experiments, select the smart chooser. If you are running a single experiment, leave it off.
4. **Start the run.** Press “Start.” You can watch progress in the step map. If you need to stop, press “Pause.” You can close laborix and resume later.
5. **Review the record and report.** When the run finishes, open the record book to see what happened. Then generate a report to share or keep for your notes.
